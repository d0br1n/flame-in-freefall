## What it is

A short entry in NASA's Spinoff database about a smoke and fire sensor first used in the space shuttle orbiters. The challenge page describes it as a 1970s detector that tells smoke from dust, giving fewer nuisance alarms, with industrial uses such as mining.

## Key facts

- **Device:** the **Brunswick Incipient Fire Detector**, originally used in space shuttle orbiters.
- **How it works:** it samples air to detect combustion particles.
- **Key advantage:** it can **discriminate between combustion particles and other matter such as dust**, so it is less prone to false alarms than earlier equipment.
- **Early warning:** it signals a fire condition **before flame and visible smoke appear** (hence "incipient"), which the entry presents as an advance over commercial smoke detectors of the time.
- **Networking:** any number of sensors can be linked to a central processor and display, giving a fire-watch system that warns at once and pinpoints the location. The entry gives mines as an example of a large-area application.

The underlying problem is the same one the Researcher's Guide raises: smoke particles in microgravity differ in size from those on Earth, so a detector tuned on the ground may look for the wrong signal. The challenge background also opens with a smoke alarm going off on Artemis II, so false alarms and reliable detection are a current concern. Lunar dust in a Moon Base or rover makes smoke-versus-dust discrimination newly important.
