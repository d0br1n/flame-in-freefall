# Resource 5: Apollo 1 (AS-204)

**Link:** https://sma.nasa.gov/sma-disciplines/accllp/apollo-1
**Type:** NASA historical and lessons-learned page
**Owner:** NASA Office of Safety and Mission Assurance, Apollo Challenger Columbia Lessons Learned Program (ACCLLP)
**Read:** 2026-11-14

## What it is

NASA's lessons-learned page on the Apollo 1 fire. It has sections for history, crew, the planned mission, the accident, the investigation, lessons learned, memorials and further resources. The challenge page lists it as a fire incident that shows why spacecraft fire safety matters.

## Key facts

### The mission

- Officially designated AS-204; planned as the first crewed Apollo flight, a 14-day Earth-orbit test of a Block I Command and Service Module, with launch scheduled for 21 February 1967.
- Crew: Virgil "Gus" Grissom, Edward White and Roger Chaffee.
- Spacecraft: Command Module 012, built by North American Aviation, delivered to Kennedy Space Center in August 1966 and moved to Launch Pad 34 in January 1967.

### The accident

- On **27 January 1967**, during a launch-pad test in which the spacecraft switched to internal power, the crew were suited, the hatches closed and the cabin pressurized with **100% oxygen**.
- A flash fire swept through the Command Module. Within seconds, rising temperature and pressure cracked the pressure vessel, releasing flame and smoke onto the pad structure.
- The crew tried to open the inner hatch while the pad crew worked on the two outer hatches. The crew died before the hatches could be opened.
- Pathologists concluded the astronauts died of **asphyxia from carbon monoxide and other toxic gases**. Their burns were likely survivable.

### The investigation

- The Apollo 204 Review Board was established on 28 January 1967 and set up 21 panels. It released a 3,000-page report on 9 April 1967. Both houses of Congress held hearings.
- **No single ignition source was determined**, but investigators found evidence of **electrical arcing in wiring** near the environmental control system, the area with the worst fire damage.
- The **pure oxygen atmosphere** made the fire spread quickly.
- The **three-piece hatch** took 90 seconds to open under ideal conditions and could not be opened against the increased cabin pressure.
- Because the rocket was unfueled, the test was classed as non-hazardous and was not reviewed by safety organizations. There was no defined rescue or medical procedure.
- The board found both technical and management lapses.

### Lessons learned and changes

- All later crews flew the Block II spacecraft.
- The hatch was replaced by a unified design that opens from inside or outside in three seconds.
- NASA **severely restricted and controlled combustible materials** in the Command Module and the Lunar Module.
- New spacesuits were designed with **fire-resistant materials**.
- Management and safety culture changes followed.
- Wiring insulation improved in the redesign; the Apollo 13 page credits this with preventing arcing when that spacecraft's cold, wet Command Module was powered back up.

## How it relates to the challenge

Apollo 1 is the defining case for the **prevention** pillar: an oxygen-rich atmosphere, an ignition source from wiring, and flammable cabin materials. Those are the same variables the challenge highlights for the planned Moon Base and pressurized rovers, which are expected to run at about 30% oxygen or more. It also shows that toxic combustion products, not flame, were the cause of death, which connects to smoke research.

| Apollo 1 factor | Related PSI investigations |
|---|---|
| Elevated oxygen concentration | ACME CFI-G, ACME BRE, BASS / BASS-II oxygen sweeps |
| Material flammability and selection | BASS, BASS-II, Saffire-I/II/III |
| Smoke and toxic gases | SAME, SAME-R |

## Caveats

- This fire occurred at normal gravity on the launch pad. It is evidence about oxygen and materials, not about microgravity flames.
- The page's accident section gives Chaffee's first name as "Robert"; elsewhere on the same page, and in the historical record, it is Roger.
- The linked investigation documents (Review Board report, congressional testimony, Phillips and Baron reports) were not read for this file.
