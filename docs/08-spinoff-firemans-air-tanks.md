## What it is

A short entry in NASA's Spinoff database, which records commercial and public uses of NASA technology. The challenge page describes it as the 1970s adaptation of spacesuit technology to improve firefighters' air tanks and breathing gear.

## Key facts

- **Partners:** NASA's Johnson Space Center and Scott Aviation, a division of A-T-O Inc.
- **Product:** a lightweight firefighter breathing system (backpack air tank and mask).
- **Weight:** about 20 pounds for a 30-minute air supply, **13 pounds lighter** than conventional firefighting tanks of the time.
- **Pressure:** 4,500 psi, **twice** that of the tanks then in use.
- **Construction:** an aluminum liner wrapped in resin-impregnated glass fibers, which removes the corrosion problem and cuts weight.
- **Mask:** redesigned for better vision.
- **Low-air warning:** personal to the wearer, so others nearby do not hear it and confusion on the fireground is reduced.
- **Further uptake:** Structural Composites Inc., Boeing and Martin-Marietta developed uses for the technology.

It supports the **optional Earth-applications angle**. The challenge page says a dashboard "could also address potential applications of the PSI data to fire safety on Earth" and names firefighter air tanks as one example of NASA technology improving terrestrial fire safety.

The link to PSI data is indirect. This spinoff came from spacesuit life-support and pressure-vessel engineering, not from combustion experiments.
