## What it is

A short entry in NASA's Spinoff database about applying spacesuit and spacecraft materials to firefighter protective clothing. The challenge page describes it as a 1980s application of NASA spacesuit technology to improve firefighter clothing.

## Key facts

- **Program:** **Project FIRES** (Firefighter's Integrated Response Equipment System).
- **Sponsors:** NASA and the U.S. Fire Administration of the Federal Emergency Management Agency, with technical management by NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center.
- **Goal:** reduce firefighter injuries and deaths by updating protective gear that did not adequately protect against many fireground hazards.
- **Approach:** apply advanced materials and design concepts derived from the Apollo program. The focus was on **lightweight, fire-resistant, heat-protective materials** first developed for astronauts' spacesuits or for spacecraft components needing thermal protection.
- **Evaluation:** fourteen municipal fire departments took part in field trials, with the data intended as a basis for new nationwide standards for protective ensembles.

## How it relates to the challenge

It supports the **optional Earth-applications angle**; the challenge page names firefighter protective clothing as an example of NASA technology improving fire safety on Earth.

There is also a line back to the historical incidents. After the Apollo 1 fire NASA restricted combustible materials and designed new spacesuits from fire-resistant materials. Those are the Apollo-derived materials this project passed on to firefighters. So the chain runs: spacecraft fire → materials research → safer spacecraft → safer firefighters.
